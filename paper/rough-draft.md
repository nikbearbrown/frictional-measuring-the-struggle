# Frictional: Measuring the Struggle Without Scoring the Learner

**Nik Bear Brown · Conceptual manuscript, rough revision · November 27, 2026**

A new reading copy of the [July 15 preprint](preprint.md), which remains unchanged. This revision presents a measurement argument and proposed research; it reports no new experiment, corpus, calibrated model, or classroom result.

## Abstract

A polished artifact can show that a deliverable meets a standard while giving little information about what its maker can do independently. This distinction matters when generative systems can supply much of a deliverable. We propose examining a second evidence stream: observations associated with a checkpoint, revision, explanation, or later performance task. Each observation is tied to a source, qualified by its scope, and interpreted against alternative explanations. We recast the original Frictional measures as candidate observations rather than ingredients of a probability of genuine learning. A synthetic benchmark can verify what an auditor detects, a separate portfolio study can examine transport, and a consent-based post-grade study can ask how instructors interpret reports. Stronger claims about competence require an independently defined performance criterion. The goal is assessment dialogue, not AI detection, misconduct inference, or assigning grades for visible struggle.

## 1. The assessment problem

An artifact can answer whether a program meets tests or whether a report supports its conclusions. A course may also ask a different question: can the learner explain a choice, transfer an idea, or repair the work when assumptions change? Human collaboration, reuse, and outside help already complicate the inference. Generative assistance sharpens the distinction because substantial work can arrive as a finished contribution without revealing the decisions behind it.

The question is not whether a deliverable was made entirely without assistance. It is when an added observation changes what a reviewer can warrant saying about a particular task. A revision receipt may establish that a test changed with a bug fix. It does not establish who understood the change. A delayed task may provide evidence about performance under its stated conditions, but cannot say which feature of an earlier assignment caused it.

## 2. Observable evidence and its limits

A process observation is a bounded statement about events or artifacts linked to work in progress. It records the source, snapshot, supported statement, and material uncertainties. Interpretations and competing explanations are separate from the observations. A check should be capable of contradicting an inference rather than merely repeating a feature of the artifact.

| Signal in the original preprint | Candidate observation | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal engagement | Timing of recorded checkpoints or saves | Not effort or time outside the recorded system |
| Error trajectory | Observed changes in failures across revisions | Not who understood them or why they happened |
| Transfer | Performance on a novel, aligned task | Not competence beyond the task and conditions |
| Uncertainty calibration | Relationship between elicited confidence and observed performance | Confidence alone is not proficiency |
| Social knowledge texture | How explicitly an explanation links to a decision or artifact | Wording is not evidence of authorship |
| Retrieval over time | Performance on comparable earlier and delayed tasks | No claim of a spacing effect without a comparison |
| Scaffolding response | Performance under documented assistance | Does not establish unaided performance |

**Table 1.** A working interpretation map, not a validated scale. The facets are distinct, unranked contexts. No component is presumed to exist in every course or belong in a composite.

Observations can be complementary without being statistically independent. Product and trace both depend on tool, workflow, and learner choices. One visible commit on a squash-merged branch is compatible with weeks of sustained work. Detailed history can coexist with limited understanding. Missing evidence is missing, not neutralized by guessing or scored as underperformance.

## 3. Worked example

**Fictional case.** A final project passes a documented acceptance test. Earlier snapshots show a failure and the test appearing with a fix. This supports one statement about the product and one about the observed record. A learner, teammate, or tool could have diagnosed the problem, and these sources cannot choose among them. If the course outcome is debugging transfer, the instructor could offer a comparable novel fault, specify what help is allowed, and assess the observed diagnosis against a rubric. This performance would be an independent check of the stated capability, not a truth label derived from the first two observations.

Avoid requiring students to manufacture a trail for the auditor. A checkpoint or rationale should have instructional purpose even if it is never audited. Provide equivalent options where disability, offline work, access constraints or team roles make one recording practice inappropriate. Avoid pervasive telemetry or emotional inference.

## 4. Research program

**Detection on controlled material.** [Auditing the Making](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/auditing-the-making) proposes synthetic cases with frozen expected facts and unavailable-evidence controls. Detection and abstention are tool outcomes, not measures of how common a behavior is or what students learned.

**Transport beyond fixtures.** [ASSUMPTION: any external portfolio study proceeds separately only after source permissions and applicable institutional determinations.] A held-out corpus can expose unfamiliar layouts and missing evidence. Selection and provenance must be documented. The corpus provides neither a consented class sample nor learner-level truth.

**Post-grade interpretation.** [Process Evidence in Practice](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/process-evidence-in-practice) proposes review only after grading on consenting submissions, subject to written institutional determination and any required approval. It asks whether a report adds useful, supported context. It does not measure grading impact or time saved.

**Separate learning validity test.** [ASSUMPTION: outside the current classroom plan.] To test whether preselected traces add information about competence, first define an aligned, independent task. Compare artifact-only and artifact-plus-trace procedures on held-out projects or courses. Use neither the target to build trace features nor those features to construct the target. Report added value, failure and differential missingness with uncertainty at the right unit. Without that criterion, stop at description and usability.

## 5. Discussion

This revision drops unsupported numeric neuroscience claims, the proposed composite probability, circular validation, and the public corpus claim. [NEEDS: a structured primary-source review on assessment validity, process evidence and transfer.] [NIST's evaluation probes](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/building-evaluation-probes-agentic-ai) illustrate a different verification problem: whether cited text supports an agent's claims. Neither source support nor repository traceability establishes learning on its own.

Frictional proposes a second inspectable source for a narrower question. It can assist a conversation only if alternative explanations remain visible and the reviewer can conclude that there is insufficient evidence.
